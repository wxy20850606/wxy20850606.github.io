# Selling My Blue Chip  
## What 12 Years of Marriage Taught Me About Value

I recently wrote about my mistakes in investing — how I sold excellent companies simply because I lost patience during long, quiet periods.

As I reflected on that pattern, I realized something far more difficult to admit:

**I applied the same flawed judgment to my marriage.**

This is not a story about betrayal or villainy.  
It is a story about **misreading value while I was changing**.

I am writing this not to punish myself, but to understand how growth, impatience, and projection distorted my perception — and how I want to live with greater clarity going forward.

---

## 1. When Growth Looks Like “Noise”

In investing, *noise* is short-term movement that distracts from fundamentals.

In my life, the noise appeared during a period of long-suppressed change.

After years as a stay-at-home mother, I had gradually lost connection with parts of myself — strength, structure, my body, creative expression. I spent nearly all my time to take care of my two kids.

Last year, I finally began to rebuild myself.
During that process, I met someone at the gym.

I admired his discipline, his consistency, his courage, his commitment to readidng and writing. We shared only a few conversations, yet the emotional intensity felt disproportionate.

Looking back, I see this clearly now:

### It is the Mirror Effect

He was not the source of my awakening.  
He reflected qualities I was in the process of developing myself.

What I experienced was not love in its mature sense, but **recognition** — the shock of seeing a possible future version of myself.

That recognition mattered. It catalyzed real change:
- disciplined strength training
- creating and writing
- rebuilding internal structure
- reclaiming agency

The growth itself was real and necessary.

The misjudgment was **confusing internal emergence with external attachment**.

---

## 2. Understanding the Misjudgment

Using Charlie Munger’s framework, I can now see the psychological forces at work:

### Liking / Loving Tendency  
Admiration created a halo effect. Certain traits were amplified beyond their actual relational meaning.

### Contrast Misreaction  
Against years of domestic routine, difference felt extraordinary. The contrast exaggerated significance.

### Misattribution  
Internal change was attributed to an external person, instead of being recognized as self-generated.

None of this makes me reckless or ungrateful.  
It makes me **human during transition**.

---

## 3. The Value I Underestimated — and Why

My husband was a long-term partner whose value compounded quietly over time.

His love was not expressed through intensity or poetry, but through action, support, reliability, and protection.

Even now, as we separate, he continues to act with generosity:
- helping me build tools for my next chapter
- giving me time to stabilize
- prioritizing my safety and future

I did not fail because I wanted to be alive.  
I failed because **I did not yet know how to integrate aliveness with commitment**.

Only this year, i found a way to stay alive in my marriage and started to amend the relationship, but the damage was caused.
---

## 4. Accountability Without Self-Erasure

I take responsibility for the impact of my misjudgment.

At the same time, I value my growth of last year despite the misjudgement of the effect of the mirrow person.

Two truths can coexist:
- My husband deserved steadiness and clarity.
- My aliveness was not wrong — only poorly integrated at the time.

Mature accountability does not require self-annihilation.  
It requires **learning without shrinking**.

---

## 5. How I Will Hold My Values Going Forward

From this experience, I am building clearer principles — not as punishment, but as structure.

### Separate Projection from Relationship  
Recognition can guide self-development, not substitute for intimacy.

### Trust Fundamentals Over Intensity  
Consistency over years outweighs excitement over moments.

### Use Time as a Filter  
Just as in investing, I will allow time — not emotion — to test what is real.

### Regulate Before Choosing  
A stable nervous system prevents the need for external stimulation.

### Build Independence First  
Future relationships—and the ongoing evolution of my current connections—will be chosen from alignment, not dependence.

---

## Closing Thought

Some positions, once closed, cannot be reopened.

That does not mean the original growth was a mistake — only that **clarity arrived after consequence**.

My husband continues to meet this ending with dignity. I intend to do the same.

Though the marriage might be ended, we are navigating a transition into a new form of kinship—one built on a decade of shared history with two kids.

Compounding rewards patience.
Extremes are where most mistakes are made.
Low expectations, calm thinking, and endurance are the foundations of long-term investing and a good life.

I learned this lesson at great cost.  
I will carry it forward.
